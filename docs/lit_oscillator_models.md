# Literature review: oscillator, resonate-and-fire, and TRF models of beat-locked EEG (for "Theory 3")

Prepared 2026-11-09. Scope: prior art for a held-out-tempo model comparison in which (a) a Hopf/Stuart-Landau oscillator bank, (b) resonate-and-fire (RF) spiking units, and (c) a linear damped-oscillator (LTI) bank are used as feature generators for a ridge encoding model of EEG recorded during naturalistic music (Weineck, Wen & Henry 2022; 13 tempi, 1-4 Hz), compared against short (0-400 ms) and long (0-1000 ms) TRF kernels under leave-one-tempo-out cross-validation, with held-out prediction r as the primary metric and a preferred-rate (resonance) test across tempi.

Sources actually queried: Consensus (6 successful searches), PubMed (search + metadata; bibliographic data below are "based on records retrieved from PubMed" unless noted), alphaXiv (3 searches), Crossref API, and doi.org resolution. Only items returned by these searches are listed; nothing is reported from memory alone.

Verification convention used below:
- **[doi.org resolves]** = a WebFetch of https://doi.org/<DOI> returned a 302 redirect to the publisher (the DOI is registered and resolves). Publisher pages themselves often returned 403 to the fetcher, so abstracts come from PubMed/Europe PMC/Crossref/PMC.
- **[PubMed record]** = DOI taken from the PubMed metadata record (treated as verified).
- **[Crossref record]** = DOI taken from the Crossref registry (treated as verified; doi.org not separately fetched).
- **[arXiv page fetched]** = abstract page retrieved; preprint, not peer reviewed unless stated.
- **[link unverified]** = not independently resolved.

## CLOSEST PRIOR ART (papers that fit or simulate a resonant/oscillator model against beat-entrainment M/EEG)

### 1. Doelling, Assaneo, Bevilacqua, Pesaran & Poeppel (2019). "An oscillator model better predicts cortical entrainment to music." PNAS 116(20):10113-10121. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1816414116 [PubMed record; PMC6525506 fetched]
- **What they did:** Simulated (not fit) a single Wilson-Cowan excitatory/inhibitory population oscillator (parameters set near an Andronov-Hopf bifurcation, resting frequency ~4 Hz, driven by the stimulus envelope) and a linear "evoked" model (envelope convolved with a fixed, participant-averaged MEG tone-response kernel). Compared both to MEG from 27 participants (re-analysis; piano clips at 1-8 notes/s) and 12 new participants (resynthesized clips, sharp vs smooth attacks).
- **Metric:** phase concentration metric (PCM): how tightly the stimulus-to-neural phase lag clusters across note rates. Evoked prediction ~0.25-0.28, oscillator ~0.58-0.64; data fell in the oscillator's range; AIC on Gaussian fits favoured the oscillator.
- **How it differs from Theory 3:** (i) qualitative simulation vs our held-out-tempo *predictive* comparison; (ii) a *single* rate-based Wilson-Cowan oscillator vs our *banks* (log-spaced 0.5-8 Hz) of Hopf, RF-spiking, and LTI units; (iii) phase-lag concentration vs held-out prediction r of the EEG time series; (iv) no cross-validation across stimulation rates; (v) evoked kernel was fixed from tone responses, not a ridge-fit TRF.
- **Relevance:** the direct ancestor of our oscillator-vs-evoked contrast and the source of the "phase lag should be rate-dependent for evoked, rate-invariant for oscillator" diagnostic, which we can report as a secondary check.

### 2. Damsma, de Roo, Doelling, Bazin & Bouwer (2025). "Tempo-dependent selective enhancement of neural responses at the beat frequency can be mimicked by both an oscillator and an evoked model." Cerebral Cortex 35(9), bhaf258. DOI 10.1093/cercor/bhaf258 [PubMed record; PMC12461700 fetched]
- **What they did:** 12 participants heard a repeating rhythmic pattern at five grid intervals (150-600 ms) while tapping/listening. EEG FFT power at grid frequency and subharmonics shifted with tempo, mirroring tapping at a preferred ~2 Hz rate. Simulated a *single* Wilson-Cowan oscillator (after Doelling 2019; kappa = 10, tau = 6.25, resting ~1.33 Hz, envelope-driven) and an evoked model (binary onset train convolved with an mTRF ridge kernel, lags 0-1000 ms, lambda = 1, estimated across all tempi then averaged). Compared z-scored model spectra to EEG spectra by MSE (Bayesian paired t-test: no decisive preference, BF10 = 1.42) and PCM (oscillator 0.80, evoked 0.23, EEG 0.41; data closer to evoked, BF10 = 2.47).
- **Conclusion:** selective enhancement at the beat/preferred rate across tempi does *not* discriminate entrainment from successive evoked responses.
- **How it differs from Theory 3:** single oscillator vs bank; spectral-shape and PCM match vs held-out time-domain prediction r; the evoked kernel was fit on all tempi (no leave-one-tempo-out); rate units only; no spiking model; no linear-oscillator control. Their evoked model is essentially our "long TRF (0-1000 ms)" baseline, which makes this the key paper to cite when motivating why a *held-out-tempo* test is needed.
- **Risk flag:** their result predicts that a tempo-generalizing TRF may match the oscillator bank, i.e. Theory 3's primary contrast may come out null.

### 3. Zalta, Large, Schon & Morillon (2024). "Neural dynamics of predictive timing and motor engagement in music listening." Science Advances 10(10):eadi2525. DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adi2525 [doi.org resolves; Europe PMC and PMC10917349 fetched]
- **What they did:** MEG (29 analysed) during 12 melodies at 3 syncopation levels with a 2 Hz beat; online groove ratings (n = 66). A three-layer GrFNN-style canonical Hopf oscillator network (321 oscillators per layer, natural frequencies log-spaced 0.375-12 Hz; layer 1 near Hopf bifurcation, layers 2-3 bistable double-limit-cycle regime; Hebbian learning layer 1 -> 2; layer 3 = prediction minus input) driven by MIDI note onsets. Not fit to MEG: the model's 2 Hz layer amplitudes were (i) regressed against behaviour (syncopation r^2 = 0.85; groove r^2 = 0.66) and (ii) used as decoding targets for whole-brain MEG power (layers 1 and 3 decodable at 2 Hz and harmonics).
- **How it differs from Theory 3:** closest to our Hopf *bank* (log-spaced natural frequencies, onset-driven), but the oscillator network was a theory-generating model whose summary outputs were decoded from MEG, not an encoding model predicting EEG with held-out stimuli; single tempo (2 Hz), so no preferred-rate test across tempi; rate units; no linear-oscillator or RF control; no TRF comparison.
- **Relevance:** establishes precedent and parameter conventions (log-spaced 0.375-12 Hz Hopf bank, onset drive) we can cite directly.

### 4. Pesnot Lerousseau, Trebuchon, Morillon & Schon (2021). "Frequency selectivity of persistent cortical oscillatory responses to auditory rhythmic stimulation." J Neurosci 41(38):7991-8006. DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0213-21.2021 [PubMed record]
- **What they did:** iEEG and MEG during melodies (60/80 Hz tones in a 2.5 Hz stream). Persistent post-stimulus activity in high gamma but *not* at 2.5 Hz. Fit a **damped harmonic oscillator** model to the neural data and classified responses into three dynamical classes with distinct damping and eigenfrequencies.
- **How it differs from Theory 3:** this is the one paper that fits a *linear damped oscillator* (our LTI bank) to human rhythm-evoked data, but as a descriptive fit of response decay, not as a stimulus-driven encoding model with held-out tempi; single oscillator per site; no spiking units; no comparison with a TRF. It is the natural citation for the LTI/damped-oscillator control and for the expectation that low-frequency (delta) responses behave like heavily damped, i.e. evoked-like, systems.

### 5. Tal, Large, Rabinovitch, Wei, Schroeder, Poeppel & Zion Golumbic (2017). "Neural entrainment to the beat: the 'missing-pulse' phenomenon." J Neurosci 37(26):6331-6341. DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2500-16.2017 [PubMed record]
- MEG during 30 s syncopated drum patterns with no linear energy at the pulse frequency; auditory-cortex power and phase locking at the missing pulse, correlated with behavioural pulse-finding speed. Framed as a test of Neural Resonance Theory; no quantitative oscillator fit to the MEG (the GrFNN simulations supporting it are in Large et al. 2015). Differs from Theory 3: detection of a stimulus-absent frequency vs predictive encoding; no cross-tempo generalisation.

### 6. Mondok & Wiener (2025). "A coupled oscillator model predicts the effect of neuromodulation and a novel human tempo-matching bias." J Neurophysiol 133(5):1607-1617. DOI 10.1152/jn.00348.2024 [PubMed record]
- EEG spectral peaks at two simultaneous tempi converge during a tempo-matching task; a phase-coupled oscillator model reproduces behavioural anchoring bias and predicts the effect of 2 Hz tACS. Model fit to behaviour, qualitatively related to EEG; single pair of oscillators; no encoding of EEG time series.

### 7. Large, Herrera & Velasco (2015). "Neural networks for beat perception in musical rhythm." Front Syst Neurosci 9:159. DOI 10.3389/fnsys.2015.00159 [PubMed record]
- GrFNN (canonical Hopf network) simulations of Nozaradan-type rhythms reproducing EEG frequency-tagging amplitude patterns qualitatively; no fit, no held-out test, no spiking units.

### 8. Lerud, Almonte, Kim & Large (2014). "Mode-locking neurodynamics predict human auditory brainstem responses to musical intervals." Hearing Research 308:41-49. DOI 10.1016/j.heares.2013.09.010 [PubMed record]
- A GrFNN fit/prediction of human brainstem FFRs (pitch range), the clearest case of a GrFNN compared quantitatively to human electrophysiology; not beat-rate, not cortical EEG.

### 9. Zemlianova, Bose & Rinzel (2024). "Dynamical mechanisms of how an RNN keeps a beat, uncovered with a low-dimensional reduced model." Sci Rep 14:26388. DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-77849-x [PubMed record] (author correction: DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-97359-8)
- E/I RNN trained on synchronisation-continuation at 2-8 Hz produces a context-controlled network oscillator matching monkey neural dynamics; reduced three-variable rate model. Relevant as a learned-oscillator alternative; not EEG, not an encoding model.

### 10. Tichko, Page, Kim, Large & Loui (2022). "Neural entrainment to musical pulse in naturalistic music is preserved in aging." Brain Sciences 12(12):1676. DOI 10.3390/brainsci12121676 [doi.org resolves; Europe PMC fetched]
- Phase-locking value to the detected pulse of self-selected naturalistic music in younger vs older adults; PLV did not differ by age. **No GrFNN/oscillator fit to the EEG** (contrary to the task brief's expectation). Searches for a Tichko/Large GrFNN fit to infant EEG returned nothing; the Tichko & Large (2019, Ann NY Acad Sci, DOI 10.1111/nyas.14050 [PubMed record]) and Tichko, Kim & Large (2021, Dev Sci, DOI 10.1111/desc.13103 [PubMed record]) GrFNN papers model behaviour, not EEG.

### Not found
- No paper was returned that fits a **Stuart-Landau/Hopf bank** as an **encoding model** of EEG with **held-out tempi**; no paper used **resonate-and-fire spiking units** as EEG feature generators; no paper compared oscillator banks to **short vs long TRF kernels** under leave-one-tempo-out CV. Lakatos's work (below) is intracranial-primate/attention, not a model fit to beat EEG.

## (a) Resonate-and-fire neurons and resonant spiking models

- **Izhikevich (2001). "Resonate-and-fire neurons." Neural Networks 14(6-7):883-894. DOI 10.1016/S0893-6080(01)00078-8** [Crossref record; doi.org resolves]. Complex-valued state (damped subthreshold oscillation) with threshold reset; neurons prefer input at their eigenfrequency, respond selectively to doublet ISIs, can fire to inhibition. Foundational model for our RF bank; its eigenfrequency parameter maps onto the log-spaced 0.5-8 Hz grid.
- **Higuchi, Kairat, Bohte & Otte (2024). "Balanced Resonate-and-Fire Neurons." arXiv:2402.14603 (ICML 2024)** [arXiv listing; alphaXiv]. Fixes RF training pathologies (divergence, reset) and shows BRF recurrent SNNs match or beat LIF RSNNs on sequence tasks with far fewer spikes. Companion: "Understanding the Convergence in Balanced Resonate-and-Fire Neurons" arXiv:2406.00389. Supports the claim that RF units extract frequency content that LIF units (low-pass) cannot.
- **Micheli, Gallicchio & Tomen (2026). "Beyond Low-Pass Dynamics: Frequency-Selective Spiking Reservoirs with Resonant Neurons." arXiv:2610.37906 (accepted NeurIPS 2026)** [arXiv page fetched]. Replaces LIF with heterogeneous Harmonic RF neurons in a liquid state machine; more discriminative representations across ten benchmarks; no EEG. Directly motivates comparing an RF bank against the LIF-based ARSPI-Net reservoir, and is the closest methodological precedent for "resonant spiking reservoir as fixed feature generator".
- **Alonso & Vilimelis Aceituno / Microsoft (2021). "Deep Spiking Neural Networks with Resonate-and-Fire Neurons." arXiv:2109.08234** [arXiv listing]. RAF-SNN trained by BPTT; no EEG.
- **Huang et al. (2024). "PRF: Parallel Resonate and Fire Neuron for Long Sequence Learning." arXiv:2410.03530**; **"Dendritic Resonate-and-Fire Neuron" arXiv:2509.17186** [arXiv listings]. RF as SSM-like long-sequence primitives; no EEG.
- **Brunel, Hakim & Richardson (2003). "Firing-rate resonance in a generalized integrate-and-fire neuron with subthreshold resonance." Phys Rev E 67:051916**; **Richardson, Brunel & Hakim (2003). "From subthreshold to firing-rate resonance." J Neurophysiol 89:2538-2554**; **Engel et al. (2008) J Neurophysiol**; **Rotstein (2017) J Comput Neurosci** [Consensus records; DOIs not fetched, link unverified]. Theory of how subthreshold resonance transfers to spiking-rate resonance under noise, which is exactly the regime that determines whether an RF bank's spike counts will show a preferred-rate effect.
- **Bittar & Garner (2024). "Exploring neural oscillations during speech perception via surrogate gradient spiking neural networks." Front Neurosci 18:1449181. DOI 10.3389/fnins.2024.1449181** [doi.org resolves; arXiv page fetched]. Trained SNN speech recogniser develops delta/theta/beta oscillations and cross-frequency coupling; no EEG comparison.
- **EEG-specific resonant spiking work: none found.** Closest: "Learning Alzheimer's Disease Signatures by bridging EEG with Spiking Neural Networks and Biophysical Simulations" arXiv:2602.07010 [alphaXiv listing only; link unverified], and Kasabov's NeuCube (below), which uses LIF, not RF.

Encoding-model lineage (stimulus-driven prediction of EEG) is covered in (d). ## (d) TRF / mTRF methods applied to music and rhythm EEG

## Gaps and risks for Theory 3 (what reviewers will say)

1. **Damsma et al. (2025) already showed a tempo-general evoked kernel mimics the oscillator.** Expect: "Your long TRF is their evoked model; if the Hopf bank does not beat it on held-out tempi, you have replicated Damsma." Mitigation: pre-register the directional prediction and the equivalence test; report Bayes factors as they did; include the Doelling PCM diagnostic as a secondary, since it is where oscillators and kernels differ most.
2. **Linear-oscillator confound.** Pesnot Lerousseau 2021 fit damped harmonic oscillators to rhythm responses; a heavily damped LTI bank is a band-pass filterbank of the stimulus and is nearly equivalent to a long TRF with a resonant basis. Reviewers will ask whether any Hopf advantage is from nonlinearity (amplitude saturation, mode locking) or just from a wider effective kernel. Mitigation: match effective impulse-response length across banks; include the LTI bank (already planned) and report Hopf minus LTI as the nonlinearity contrast.
3. **Stimulus-spectrum confound (Henry 2017; Rajendran & Schnupp 2019; Lopez-Madrona 2026).** A preferred-rate effect in held-out r can arise because spectral flux/onset features themselves have tempo-dependent spectra. Mitigation: report the model-free tempo profile of the stimulus features and of the EEG-feature coherence; show the resonance effect survives after regressing out stimulus power at the beat frequency.
4. **Weineck 2022 found monotonic (slower = stronger) tracking, not a peak.** A resonance claim must be reconciled with the original monotonic gradient; a Hopf bank with 0.5-8 Hz natural frequencies may simply reproduce low-pass behaviour. Pre-specify what constitutes a "peak" vs a gradient and the test for it.
5. **RF-spiking bank adds parameters (threshold, reset, noise) with little EEG precedent.** No prior work uses RF units for EEG encoding; the theory papers (Brunel 2003; Richardson 2003; Rotstein 2017) show firing-rate resonance depends strongly on noise level. Reviewers will ask for a sensitivity analysis over noise/threshold and for a LIF-reservoir control (the existing ARSPI-Net) to isolate the resonance ingredient.
6. **Leave-one-tempo-out with 13 tempi and 34 participants** gives modest statistical power for interactions; tempo spacing (1-4 Hz) covers only part of the 0.5-8 Hz bank. Interpolation vs extrapolation (held-out 1 Hz or 4 Hz) should be reported separately.
7. **Mode locking / subharmonics (Kim & Large 2019; Damsma 2025).** Fast tempi shifted EEG energy to subharmonics; a bank will lock at k:m ratios. Decide in advance whether subharmonic locking counts as "preferred-rate" evidence.
8. **Zalta 2024 precedent.** Reviewers from the NRT community will note that a 321-unit Hopf bank with Hebbian layers has already been compared to MEG; our contribution must be framed as the *held-out-tempo encoding* test, not as introducing Hopf banks to M/EEG.
9. **IEEE TNSRE fit.** The SNN-EEG literature in TNSRE/TBME/TNNLS is almost entirely classification; a measurement-instrument framing is unusual there, so the paper needs an explicit statement of what the spiking stage buys over a linear/Hopf front-end (e.g. event-driven implementation, or a demonstrated predictive gain).
